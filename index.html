Unsupervised Learning · MovieLens 33M

Hybrid Movie Recommendation via Clustering & Behavioral Profiles

Built on the MovieLens 33M dataset — titles, genres, and ratings only, no genome-tag scores. The goal: group movies by genre similarity with no supervision at all, then use those clusters as the backbone of a behavioral profile for every user, and let that profile drive a rule-based recommendation strategy. Movies released before 2015 trained the model; everything from 2015 onward was held out as validation, simulating new titles arriving after deployment.

Turning ratings into a behavior profile

Every user's rating history was reduced to three axes — diversity (how many of the 30 clusters they explore), generosity (z-scored average rating, to cancel out users who simply rate everything highly), and frequency (total ratings, normalized). Splitting each axis at its median sorts every one of the ~200,947 users into one of 8 octants: {Eclectic|Focused} × {Generous|Critical} × {Frequent|Casual}.

Recommendation logic

Focused

Recommend only within the user's preferred clusters.

Eclectic

Mix preferred clusters with movies from outside them, for exploration.

Frequent

Gets a larger list — 20 movies.

Casual

Gets a shorter list — 10 movies.

Ranking is built per cluster from average rating, filtered to movies with 100+ ratings. It's a simple business-rules layer over the segmentation — not a trained supervised model — but it shows how unsupervised structure can feed straight into personalization.

Applied Regression · Kaggle Airbnb 2025

Short-Stay Price Prediction — Global Model vs. Region-Sensitive Models

Predicting nightly Airbnb prices for 200,000+ listings worldwide, moving through cleaning, geographic exploration, and iterative tree-ensemble modeling. The core question: is one global model using latitude/longitude as features enough, or does training separate models per region — Europe, America, Australia — capture market-specific pricing dynamics that a single model misses?

Columns with more than 15% missing values were dropped, then remaining rows with any missing values (208,757 → 180,695 rows). Plotly scatter plots of longitude vs. latitude — before and after dropping NaNs — showed the cleanup was quietly cutting out parts of Western Australia, the Mediterranean, and the US, which is what motivated filtering into regional bounding boxes: Europe (lat 20–80, long -50–50), America (lat -40–80, long -150–-50), Australia (lat -50–-10, long 100–150).

LightGBM matched Random Forest's accuracy at a fraction of the training time, so it carried the rest of the investigation. Feature-importance analysis flagged id and host_id with suspiciously high importance — data leakage from identifier columns, not real signal — so both were dropped, while latitude and longitude stayed in as genuinely predictive geography.

Hyperparameters were then swept step by step: n_estimators 50/75/100/300/500/600 (best at 500–600, diminishing returns beyond), max_depth 2/4/8/10/12 (little gain past 8), num_leaves 32/42/64/86/128 (settled at 64), learning_rate 0.01/0.03/0.05/0.1/0.2 (settled at 0.1).

03 — Region Sensitive: one LightGBM per market

Each region got its own LightGBM (objective='poisson' — a better fit than default regression for a strictly positive, right-skewed target like price — 500 estimators, learning_rate 0.2, num_leaves 64, max_depth 8), plus two categorical features the global model didn't use: room_type and property_type, label-encoded for LightGBM.

Key findings

  • Tree ensembles win outright — Random Forest and LightGBM massively outperform Linear Regression and single Decision Trees on this task.
  • LightGBM was the best accuracy/speed trade-off and carried the rest of the investigation.
  • Geography dominates — latitude/longitude are consistently the strongest predictors, ahead of bedrooms, bathrooms, or any physical listing attribute.
  • Identifier columns leak — id and host_id inflated feature importance without carrying real signal, and had to be caught and dropped.
  • Region-sensitive models win where there's enough data — Europe and America beat the global model on both RMSE and R²; Australia, with a far smaller training set, lands roughly on par.
